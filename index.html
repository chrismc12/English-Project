01 — The Big Picture

Why AI ethics is everybody's problem

AI is now woven into schools, hospitals, banks, social media, cars, and police departments. It can make life faster and easier — but it also collects our data, shapes our opinions, and sometimes makes unfair decisions that affect millions of people. The problem: most of us use AI every day without understanding the risks. This project breaks down the biggest ethical issues so you can think critically about how AI should be used.

03 — The Balance

The promise and the peril

Good ethics means seeing both sides. The same technology can lift people up or leave them behind — often at the same time.

The Promise

How AI can help society

  • +Faster medicine. AI can spot patterns in scans and lab results, helping doctors catch diseases like cancer earlier.
  • +Access for everyone. Free tutoring, translation, and tools for people with disabilities are suddenly available to anyone with a phone.
  • +Safer, smarter systems. AI helps manage traffic, predict storms, detect fraud, and reduce repetitive busywork.
  • +New kinds of jobs. The WEF projects 170 million new roles by 2030 — more than the number it expects to disappear.
The Peril

How AI can harm society

  • !Hidden bias. If the data is unfair, the AI is unfair — and it can deny loans, jobs, or freedom at scale.
  • !Surveillance & data grabs. Systems quietly collect, store, and sell intimate details about your life.
  • !Misinformation. Deepfakes and AI text make convincing lies cheap and fast, eroding trust in what we see.
  • !Displaced workers. 92 million existing jobs are projected to be displaced — and the new jobs need different skills.
04 — The Open Question

Should governments regulate AI more strictly?

This is one of the biggest unsettled debates in tech today. Here are the strongest arguments on each side — then cast your own vote.

Yes — regulate

  • Rules can require companies to test for bias before AI decides on jobs, loans, or arrests.
  • Laws like the EU AI Act ban the most dangerous uses and demand human oversight for high-risk systems.
  • Clear standards protect privacy and force companies to label AI-generated content and deepfakes.
  • Without guardrails, harm falls hardest on people with the least power to fight back.

No — go lighter

  • Heavy rules can slow innovation and push research to countries with fewer protections.
  • Technology changes faster than laws, so regulations risk being outdated the moment they pass.
  • Big companies can afford compliance; strict rules may crush smaller startups and competition.
  • Some argue industry standards and transparency can manage risk without government control.

05 — Takeaway

We can't stop AI. We can decide how it's used.

The point of this project isn't to make you fear AI or worship it — it's to make you a sharper, more critical user and citizen. Every recommendation feed, chatbot, and facial scan is the result of human choices. The more people who understand the ethics, the better those choices get.

"Many people use AI every day without realizing how it can influence opinions, collect data, and make unfair decisions. Understanding it is the first step to using it responsibly."
